02 · Strategy

Three gates. One rule.

Every entry needs three things to agree. If any one is missing, there is no trade. Scroll to watch the rule play out.

The long checklist

  2. The higher timeframe confirms the same direction.

  3. Hull Suite prints a red ribbon.
All three: enter short
Indicator 01

Hull Suite by InSilico

A Hull Moving Average drawn as a ribbon. It is built from weighted averages in a way that cuts the lag of an ordinary moving average while staying smooth. The ribbon prints green while it slopes up and red while it slopes down. The charts in this report use a length of 55.

Its job here: the momentum filter. If the ribbon disagrees with the signal, the trade is skipped.

Indicator 02

HalfTrend

A trend indicator that tracks a midline between recent highs and lows, buffered by volatility. It only flips when price breaks decisively through that line, then prints a Buy or Sell label at the turn.

Its job here: the trigger. It tells me when to look, and it never decides a trade alone.

Why it holds up

Not magic. Structure.

01

Confluence filters noise

One indicator on its own fires constantly and is often wrong. Requiring a signal, a higher timeframe and a ribbon to agree removes many weak entries, because noise rarely lines up three times in a row.

02

It respects the bigger trend

The higher timeframe check keeps each trade aligned with the larger flow of the market, so I am not fighting the tide on a small 15 minute wiggle.

03

Rules are objective

Each gate is either printed on the chart or it is not. That removes hunches, hesitation and revenge trades, and it makes every trade easy to review afterwards.

04

Risk is set before entry

Stop and target are mapped first, and the size is chosen so a stop out costs a fixed, pre-decided amount. A single loss is a small, known number rather than a surprise.

05

It is repeatable and testable

The same rules apply to every pair, so results can be journaled, measured and improved. A hunch cannot be tested; a rule set can.

06

Patience is part of the edge

Most of the time the three gates are not aligned, so most of the time the correct action is nothing. Skipping weak setups protects capital for the strong ones.

Where it struggles

  • Sideways markets. Signals can flip back and forth with no follow through, producing small losses.
  • Lag. Confirmation costs a little of the move. A cleaner entry often comes at a slightly worse price.
  • Small sample. Six demo days show the process working. They do not prove it works over months. That is exactly why the next step matters.
